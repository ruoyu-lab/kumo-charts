# Indicators

The indicators window is a small window that does not cover the chart: holding its left column or an empty part of its top drags it anywhere, and closed and opened again, it is where it was. The left column is split by dividers into three parts: All; Favorites and Indicator templates; Trend, Bands, Oscillators, Volume, Volatility and Order flow (with an "Other" group as well when the page has indicators of its own without a group). At the top right are the title of the category chosen and the close button, with the search box below (on the indicator templates page, the template's name and a save button). The window lists all 87 indicators of the library's indicators module (registered on every chart), the two volume profiles of the open-source library (`@kumo-charts/core/volume-profile`: VPVR for the visible range, VPSV per day), and the commercial edition's order flow: 3 indicators (when the datafeed has `getTradeMinutes()`), large trades and the order book heatmap (when the datafeed has `subscribeBook()`):

Each indicator can be found in the indicators window by its code or by its name in any language, and its parameters, lines and reference lines are changed in its settings window:

- Indicators computed from one price (the moving averages, including DEMA, TEMA, LSMA, MCGINLEY and RIBBON; BBI, ALLIGATOR, BOLL, KC, ENVELOPE, VSTOP, MACD, RSI, STOCHRSI, WR, CCI, ROC, BIAS, DMA, TRIX, MTM, DPO, PSY, FISHER, AO, PPO, CMO, KST, COPPOCK, TSI, CRSI, STD, BBW, BBP and VWAP) can pick the price: close, open, high, low, (H+L)/2, (H+L+C)/3, (O+H+L+C)/4. FISHER, AO and ALLIGATOR default to (H+L)/2, and VWAP to (H+L+C)/3.
- Parameters with choices are drop-downs or segmented buttons: the type of PIVOTS (traditional, Fibonacci, Woodie, classic, DeMark, Camarilla) and the period of PIVOTS, PREVHLC and ADR (day, week, month, quarter, year, by the symbol's trading calendar). Methods take the number of the choice: `addIndicator('PIVOTS', { params: { kind: 1, period: 1 } })` is weekly Fibonacci pivot points.
- RIBBON (moving average ribbon) is four moving averages, each with its own type (SMA, EMA, SMMA (RMA), WMA, VWMA) and length, by default SMAs of 20, 50, 100 and 200; to do without one, turn it off on the Style tab.
- PIVOTHL (pivot points high low) has a number of bars on the left and on the right for highs and for lows (10 each by default), and each pivot gets a triangle on its bar with its price written.
- ZIGZAG (zig zag) joins the turning points of the price: its parameters are the deviation (%, 5 by default), the depth (10 by default, half of the bars on each side confirming), extending to the latest bar, and what the labels say: the price, the change (absolute or in percent) and the cumulative volume (from after the turning point before up to this one), all on by default. The last turning point is replaced by one further in the same direction as the market moves, and the line and labels follow. The labels of highs are above, in the theme's up color, and those of lows below, in the down color, all drawn within the plot area, without covering the price scale; the Style tab has a single "Zig Zag" line, whose color, width and style can be changed, and turning it off draws neither the line nor the labels. Computed on another timeframe, turning points are drawn where the bars of that timeframe close, and the volume adds up the bars of that timeframe.
- Horizontal segments (pivot points, previous high, low and close, ADR) can have their color, width and style changed like lines. The boxes of trading sessions (the trading hours of the Tokyo, London and New York stock markets, each in its own time zone and daylight saving time) can each have their color changed and be hidden, and the settings show the hours of each session.
- The names of lines are shown in the interface's language: in the legend, on the Style tab, in the alert window and in the texts of alerts, Bollinger Bands are basis, upper and lower, and MACD is histogram, MACD and signal; codes such as MA9, %K and R1 are the same in every language, and the page's own indicators use the names they give. The `label` of the chart's `chart.indicators.valuesAt()` is the name in the interface's language too.
- An indicator on the main pane has "Include in auto scale" on its Style tab: off, its lines take no part in the auto scale of the price scale, and when they are far from the price they are drawn off the chart rather than squashing the bars. It is off by default for pivot points, previous high, low and close, and the boxes of trading sessions (R5, S5 and the like are far from the price), and on for the others. Methods call it `autoscale`: `true`, `false`, or `null` for the indicator's default.
- The parameters of volume profiles (VPVR, VPSV) have "Split by": **Bar direction** (the default, as the open-source library draws them, each row in two colors by bars closing up and closing down) or **Aggressor** (as the order flow of the commercial edition draws them). By aggressor, the volume of each row comes from trades (the volume bought and sold by aggressors at each price of each minute): the first part (along the edge it grows from) is the aggressive buying and the rest the aggressive selling, with the whole length still the total volume; the point of control and the value area are computed from the totals, as in the open-source edition; the number of rows, the value area, the width and the placement apply as before, and during a replay only what has been played counts. The two colors on the Style tab become the buy volume and the sell volume (the theme's buy and sell colors by default), and switching carries the two colors set before over by position. Aggressor can be chosen only when the datafeed has `getTradeMinutes()`; otherwise the choice is greyed out, with the reason below it.
  - The trades are shared with footprints (and by the charts of one symbol), fetched from the latest backward; a profile (a day, per trading day) whose trades are not all in is not drawn, and its place says "Loading", "No trades" or "Failed" (tried again shortly), with the reason in a line in the chart's bottom left corner. Just after the chart opens, the trades of the current minute can be completed from history only once it has ended, so a profile with the latest bar can take a minute or so to come. When the datafeed gives only the trades of a recent stretch, a range with earlier bars cannot be drawn, with a hint to zoom in to the latest bars.
  - Trades of at most 3 days are used: a visible range beyond it is not drawn and says "Range too long"; per trading day, only the last 3 days are drawn, and earlier days are marked so. Timeframes of seconds cannot be split by aggressor (trades are kept by the minute).
  - Methods call it `params: { split: 1 }` (`0` is by bar direction), with the lines `buy`, `sell` and `poc`; it is kept in the state in the `params` of the `indicators` of each chart. The commercial license key covers it.
- The **Order flow** group is the 3 indicators of the commercial edition's order flow computed from the aggressor side of each trade, each in a pane of its own below, and like other indicators they have legend values and buttons and a settings window, can move to other panes, and are kept in layouts and indicator templates:
  - **VD Volume Delta** (成交量差 / 出来高デルタ / 거래량 델타): a candle for each bar, from 0 to the bar's aggressive buy volume less its aggressive sell volume, with a high and a low at the highest and lowest the difference reached minute by minute; a reference line at 0. The legend shows the four values O, H, L and C.
  - **CVD Cumulative Volume Delta** (累计成交量差): volume delta added up from the start of each period, each candle from the running total at the start of its bar to the one at its end, with highs and lows by the minute; each period starts again from 0. The period is the parameter **Anchor Period** (`anchor`, with the same choices and names as the parameter of VWAP): **Session** (`0`, the default, the trading days of the symbol's trading calendar), **Week** (`1`) and **Month** (`2`), cut by the symbol's trading calendar; the legend writes it after the name (`CVD Week`). There is no quarter or year: those are trades of more than 30 days. A bar counts in the period it starts in, and when bars are as long as the period or longer (daily bars by session), each starts from 0.
  - **UDV Up/Down Volume**: the aggressive buy volume drawn above 0 and the aggressive sell volume below 0 (in the theme's up and down colors), with the difference as a "—".
  - Candles are colored up and down like columns, with "In the colors of rising and falling bars" on the Style tab; a color set is used for all of them. They have no parameters.
  - The trades are shared with footprints and with volume profiles split by aggressor; only the bars on the screen are fetched, the latest first, with only the volume bought and sold of each minute, from trades of at most 30 days (at most 30 bars on the daily timeframe; CVD fetches from the start of its period, and a period starting more than 30 days back is not drawn and says "Range too long", as the last day of a 31-day month). A bar is drawn only when the trades of each of its minutes are all in; CVD needs every minute of the period from its start to the end of the bar, and when one minute is missing, nothing after it in the period is drawn: **it never draws a running total that starts midway**. Bars not drawn say "Loading", "No trades", "Failed" or "Range too long" in the pane, with the reason in a line in the chart's bottom left corner; timeframes of seconds, and bars not by time such as Renko and Kagi, are not drawn, and say so too. Just after the chart opens, the latest bar is drawn once the current minute has ended and been completed from history.
  - When live trades come, only the latest minute is read again and only the latest bar computed again; during a replay, only the bars played are computed.
  - The trades they read exist only on the chart, not for the alerts watched in the background, so no alerts can be put on them (the legend has no bell for them, and the alert window does not list them).
  - Methods call it `addIndicator('CVD')`; when the datafeed has no `getTradeMinutes()`, the window does not list them and `addIndicator` throws. The commercial license key covers them.

- **Large trades** (LARGETRADES) and the **order book heatmap** (BOOKHEATMAP) are in the "Order flow" group, drawn by the commercial edition's order flow. Like volume profiles they are drawn on the main pane, with no pane of their own and no alerts; a chart draws only the first shown of each. The legend has no row for them: they are listed in the "Chart elements" panel, where their settings open.
  - **Large trades**: bubbles over the bars of the main pane, at the time of the trade (within the width of its bar, the start of the bar on the left) and its price, with an area by the trade's value (price × size) and a color by the aggressor side (the theme's buy and sell colors by default). Parameters: **Threshold** (`threshold`), either **Auto** (`0`, the "Count" trades with the largest value among the bars on the screen) or **Minimum value** (`1`, every trade worth at least the "Minimum value", in the quote currency); **Count** (`count`, 50 by default, 1 to 500); **Minimum value** (`minValue`, 100000 by default); **Size** (`size`, the radius of the largest bubble in pixels, 18 by default, 4 to 60). The lines are `buy` and `sell`. "Chart elements" shows the count (auto) or `≥ minimum value`. The trades are shared with footprints (and by the charts of one symbol): with `getTradeMinutes()` in the datafeed, the time before the chart opened is filled in (the 32 trades of each minute with the largest value), and without it only what comes after it opened; the large trades of a bar are drawn once its trades are all in, from the trades of the last 3 days at most; timeframes of seconds are not drawn (trades are kept by the minute), and during a replay only what has been played is drawn; why something cannot be drawn is written in the chart's bottom left corner. Kept by the minute, only the 32 trades of each minute with the largest value stay: when a minute has more than 32 trades worth more than the threshold set, the threshold actually drawn rises above the smallest trade kept for that minute, so that not a single trade worth more than the threshold drawn is missed (`chart.largeTrades.status().threshold` is how small it actually draws).
  - **Order book heatmap**: behind the bars of the main pane, each column of pixels is the average resting size of its stretch of time, and each row of pixels the row with the most resting orders among its prices, bids in the theme's buy color and asks in its sell color, more opaque as there are more (full at the 99th percentile of the cells on the screen); the right edge draws the current order book (its longest row taking 12% of the width of the plot area). Parameters: **Opacity** (`opacity`, the opacity when full, in percent, 80 by default); **Current book** (`profile`, `1` to show, `0` to hide). The lines are `buy` (bids) and `sell` (asks). The heatmap starts from the subscription to the order book (exchanges do not give past ones), and the seconds of a disconnection stay empty; with no order book yet, the chart's bottom left corner says it fills in from now on, and on a disconnection that the order book stopped coming.
  - Methods call them `addIndicator('LARGETRADES', { params: { threshold: 1, minValue: 250000 } })` and `addIndicator('BOOKHEATMAP', { params: { opacity: 60 } })`; giving a pane throws. The commercial license key covers them.

**Indicator templates** are under "Indicator templates" on the left of the indicators window: type a name and save, and the indicators on the current chart, with their parameters, lines and reference lines, are kept as a template (one of the same name is replaced after a prompt); clicking a template replaces the indicators of the current chart with the template's (the indicators there are removed, while compared symbols and drawings stay); the trash can removes a template. Templates are listed by name, and the search box finds them by name too. Templates are kept in the state's `indicatorTemplates` (`[{ name, indicators }]`, with `indicators` in the format of each chart's `indicators`), shared by every chart, and go along with `storage` or `getState()`.
